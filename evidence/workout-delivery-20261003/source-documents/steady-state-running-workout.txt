RUNNING WORKOUT
Long steady runs might not look flashy, but they’re a game-changer for boxers. These runs improve your aerobic endurance, which is the foundation of your stamina in the ring. By training your heart and lungs to work more efficiently, you'll recover faster between rounds and maintain your pace deep into the fight.
Beyond endurance, long runs strengthen your mental toughness. They teach you to stay focused and push through discomfort — a mindset that every boxer needs when the going gets tough.
Start the workout with a dynamic full-body warm-up.
Ankle circles, calves stretch, leg swings forward and to the sides, hip openers, 90-90s, high knee run, buttkicks, feet kick outs forward and backward, deep controlled squats, wall sit, shoulder circles, arm swings vertical and horizontal, elbow circles, wrists circles, neck twists and turn, torso bends, torso circles, torso twist, floor touches, cossack squats.

The main part of the workout includes running with a moderate pace (around 135-145 heart beat rate) for 30 minutes with no breaks. 

After the run you should be a bit tired and that is a perfect time to shadow box. Go for 3 rounds of 3 minutes with one minute of rest in between the rounds. Focus on staying relaxed, being your flow and keeping it realistic.

Static stretches: finish off your workout with a series of static stretches to help your muscles recover and prevent any tightness.
Wide fold, calves stretch, quad stretch, knee over toes lunge position, side lunge hold.

Remember to listen to your body and adjust the intensity as needed. Keep challenging yourself and enjoy the progress you'll make!
